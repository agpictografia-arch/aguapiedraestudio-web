Warmth for cramps.

A heating pad or warm compress on your lower belly may ease period cramps. In small studies, warmth reduced pain more than no heat. Keep it warm, not hot, so it doesn't irritate your skin.

Jo J, Lee SH. Sci Rep 2018;8:16252. doi:10.1038/s41598-018-34303-z
LutealPicked from your logs

The steadier part.

After ovulation, this part of your cycle tends to last about the same each time: around 12 days, in a large study. When your cycle runs longer or shorter, it's usually the days before ovulation that change.

Bull JR et al. npj Digit Med 2019;2:83. doi:10.1038/s41746-019-0152-7

Hot flashes.

A hot flash is a sudden feeling of heat in the face, neck and chest that can last a few minutes. Light clothing, a fan or a cold drink may help.

NHS, Menopause symptoms; NHS, Things you can do for menopause symptoms

Questions

Why not just Apple Health?

Apple Health keeps your records, and Marelle uses it as the home for your periods. Marelle adds what Health doesn't: your phase today, a range for your next period that adapts to you, guidance with sources, and a view made for the years when cycles start to change. And it keeps all of it at hand in a simple way: one look at your widget, one tap to log.

Is Marelle a medical app?

No. Marelle doesn't diagnose anything and isn't a method of birth control. It shows what your own logs say and suggests talking with your doctor when that may help. It also makes a PDF summary of your cycles and symptoms to bring to your appointment.

What if my cycles are changing?

Marelle shows a wider range for your next period and tells you why, compared with your own history. You can log hot flashes, night sweats and sleep changes next to your periods, and they go into the PDF for your appointment.

Can my daughter use it too?

Yes, on her own iPhone. From her first cycles, Marelle builds her history with her own data. The more she logs, the more it can show her, and after three cycles her phases appear. Her data stays on her phone, not on yours.
